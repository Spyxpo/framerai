The transformer architecture revolutionized natural language processing by introducing self-attention mechanisms.

This allowed models to process input sequences in parallel rather than sequentially, leading to significant improvements in both training speed and model performance.

Key innovations include multi-head attention, positional encodings, and layer normalization.

The original transformer paper "Attention is All You Need" demonstrated state-of-the-art results on machine translation tasks.

Since then, transformers have become the foundation for modern language models including BERT, GPT, and T5.

These models have achieved remarkable results across a wide range of natural language understanding and generation tasks.

The architecture continues to evolve with variants like sparse attention, efficient transformers, and mixture-of-experts approaches.
